# You've Got Mail

A pigeon delivers transparent PNGs. The Android test edition receives real parcels from one-use invitations. The [web preview](https://nikki0radz.github.io/You-ve-got-mail/) also supports local design tests.

[Open the screen board](https://pigeon-post-delivery.nikki0radz.chatgpt.site/workroom/design/board.html) to open any page or picker. Options appear above the full phone. Choose one, edit and comment, then **Export for chat** and attach the JSON for the next revision.

## Try it on Android

1. Install the signed APK on Android 9 or later. Android 0.1.41 opens **Set up your deliveries** on its first launch (and once after updating an older installation). The guide checks notifications, display over other apps and precise schedules, then offers optional background battery access. Each step opens the real Android permission screen and checks the current status when you return. Back, Skip for now and Later keep granting access optional. A final check lists anything still off. Repeat it from **Settings → Phone & appearance → Delivery setup**. Camera access is requested only when the camera is used; selecting photos needs no permission to the whole gallery.
2. Tap **Send test** and switch apps. The local test starts five seconds later. With warnings on, the incoming notification offers **Reject** for your selected 3, 10 or 15 seconds before the artwork appears. Dismiss and Reply appear on the overlay after one second.
3. Under **Settings → Send me something**, create a link and share it with a friend. They open it in their browser, choose a frame or sticker, fit a photo, position it on the portrait preview, and optionally add a note or sticker. They can send immediately or pick a time within 30 days. Their receipt page can check for your reply.
4. In **Inbox**, open received mail, send a sticker or text reply, or cancel a scheduled local parcel. Use **Sent** to check friend deliveries and replies. Under **Your people → Friends**, exchange codes and accept requests. Pick a design on the home wheel to send it to yourself or an accepted friend, now or later.

Sharing an invitation automatically starts a temporary live listening session. Android also checks periodically and on app resume; there is no manual listening control in Settings. Android 0.1.45 registers its Firebase token with the existing delivery service and receives FCM wake messages. A received push queues durable WorkManager mailbox sync immediately (expedited for high priority); the periodic check remains a recovery fallback. Active temporary listening stops once push registration is confirmed. Scheduled parcels already downloaded to the phone use native alarms. Exact timing requires alarm access. Android may defer background checks or restrict overlays on protected screens. In Phone & appearance, choose Blur or Black and set the black backdrop opacity (25% by default). Android uses this same black backdrop when cross-window blur is unavailable. In **Notification settings**, choose full pop-ups or notifications while open, and inbox-only or completely closed while closed. With full pop-ups enabled, pending deliveries can open one second after unlock. Friend-status notifications are opt-in.

All 30 reminder templates start with the original illustrated wording. Tap the reminder to replace that wording directly, or add a separate note. The guest link offers the four selected reminders. Transparent studio exports start at 1440 × 2688; photo-heavy parcels may reduce colour precision or dimensions to fit the service limit.

The in-app camera has a labelled flash switch for each lens, off by default. Android 0.1.43 requests the app window’s full brightness, covers the native app and system-bar backgrounds with a pale warm white, and acknowledges the window update before the camera starts its 1.1-second exposure settling period (about 1.5 seconds total flash, including acknowledgement and a fresh camera frame). The flash stays on for another 0.3 seconds after capture. This acknowledges Android applying the request, not a physical brightness measurement. Older APKs retain their previous brightness bridge; browsers can only light the page. Missing or rejected acknowledgements show an error without saving a photo. Prior brightness and bar colours restore after capture, cancellation, pause, destruction, and a five-second safety timeout. No additional permission is needed. Device display limits and settings such as Samsung Extra brightness or Extra dim can still affect the result. Back flash briefly enables the camera light when the device exposes torch control; unsupported camera views show N/A and still take photos with flash off. The rear LED colour is controlled by the phone.

**Settings → Phone & appearance → Natural beauty filter** defaults on and remembers an explicit off choice. It uses local face landmarks for subtle smoothing that excludes the eyes and lips, and slightly reduces a yellow cast only within the open mouth. It preserves the captured photo’s resolution and processes frames in a dedicated worker. The model loads only when the camera is used with the filter enabled; preview work uses smaller frames. If graphics or model loading is unavailable, the camera explicitly shows that the original photo is being used. Tracking, exposure and maximum physical screen brightness still need testing on the target phone.

The pinned MediaPipe runtime and model are downloaded by `scripts/prepare-beauty-assets.mjs` on the first clean build and included in the app and published design release. Generated vendor binaries stay out of Git; the downloader checks the model checksum and includes the upstream licence.

The camera’s Zoom out / Zoom in slider changes framing before capture on either camera. Masked frames can zoom out far enough to show the whole camera image instead of forcing it to fill a mismatched opening; uncovered space stays transparent. Plain photos start at the full camera view and allow digital zoom in. The saved image uses the same framing at capture resolution, including transparent PNG padding when zoomed out, so the photo-fitting step retains the chosen composition. This is digital framing within the current camera lens, not an ultra-wide lens switch.

Android 0.1.46 adds friend profiles with the last reported mail availability, delivery settings, small profile pictures and app check-in time. A background check-in is not an online indicator. The home screen has a full-width Send button for its centred category. Delivery receipts distinguish server acceptance, phone collection, notification posting (including a historical locked-phone notification), automatic display and explicit opening. Seen timestamps are queued durably when offline, and new reply versions have separate receipts. Clearing unread badges sends no opening receipt. Both phones need 0.1.46 for the new opening reports; earlier messages receive a seen time only when actually opened after updating. Native rendering and offline-to-online sync still need physical-phone verification.

Android 0.1.47 treats absent reply text and image fields separately, refreshes picture replies when opened, and repairs old null reply entries only after checking their authoritative receipt. Real notes that literally say null are retained. Missing cached picture files are downloaded again. The category carousel restores its selected category after navigation, backgrounding and resizing. Your people also clears obsolete saved heading colours, retaining friend-card colours and review comments. Browser regression coverage includes 320/430px return cycles and picture recovery; Android behaviour still needs a physical-phone check.

Saved replies now open from phone storage before using the network. Refresh reply and recovery of missing pictures still fetch the receipt. The reply loader uses three moving dots, and received artwork is revealed only after the full-size viewer styles, transparent-padding trim and final image decode are ready. Full artwork preparation runs independently of the thumbnail queue. Send confirmations and received replies keep their two footer actions on one row. These are compatible bridge-1 screen updates and require no additional APK.

Android 0.1.48 collects and acknowledges new incoming mail before syncing friend settings or old replies. Sent means accepted by the server and awaiting phone confirmation; Delivered means received on the phone; Seen means the artwork opened visibly on an unlocked screen, including an automatic pop-up or an opened notification/mailbox item. A locked notification alone remains Delivered. The earliest confirmed popup/opening time is retained. Delivery acknowledgements wake the sender once; visible confirmation/detail panels and mailbox receipts refresh every three seconds, pausing background polling and stopping completed/closed panels. Push registration is not a live connectivity indicator. Physical-phone timing and pop-up receipts still need verification.

Profile-picture sharing queues the latest saved selection while encoding/uploading and retries failed uploads on resume, reconnection and foreground checks. My profile reports sharing success, a pending retry, or the need to update an older APK. An open friend profile updates its photo when a fresh friend snapshot arrives. Existing Android 0.1.48 supports this compatible screen update; no new APK is required. Profile photos remain restricted to accepted friends by the existing server API.

## Guest delivery

The Android app creates a private mailbox key on the device. The HTTPS delivery service stores hashed credentials, claims a link atomically, and clears the parcel from the server after the receiver saves and acknowledges it. Invitations expire after seven days. The invitation and reply receipt travel in URL fragments on the guest page. Anyone holding an unspent invitation can use it once. Friend mail uses mutually accepted connections. The app stores received history on the device and saves a private receipt for each sent friend parcel.

## Development

Android 0.1.35 checks for published screen updates when opened or resumed. Compatible HTML, styles, scripts and built-in artwork download automatically, with a checksum for every file. The app keeps a complete offline copy and applies a new design only when no editor, picker or design review is open. Interrupted downloads keep the current release, and an updated screen that fails to start falls back to the previous working design. Installing 0.1.35 enables this; later compatible screen changes need no new APK. Native features and permission changes still require an installer.

`npm run build` generates `dist/design-update.json` from the exact web output. Publish the complete output at the existing Delivery workroom, including that manifest. Its bridge version must match the app's supported native interface. Never edit a published manifest without publishing its matching files.

Run `npm test`, `npm run check`, and `npm run build`. Serve the repository root or built dist folder with a static HTTP server. The PNG sprite sheets, alpha masks and template writing areas are in `assets/post`. The site uses GitHub Pages.

For Android, use JDK 17, SDK platform 35, Build Tools 35.0.0, zip, and Node.js. Set `ANDROID_HOME`, `MAIL_KEYSTORE` and `MAIL_PASSWORD_FILE`, then run `android/build.sh /path/to/Youve-Got-Mail.apk`. The Gradle wrapper resolves the pinned Firebase Messaging and AndroidX WorkManager SDKs and merges their actual manifest components. The builder bundles the same local editor, builds an optimized release, signs the APK with the private pigeonpost key, verifies its signature, and checks that the packaged Firebase receiver, registration worker and client configuration are present. It normalizes ZIP compression without changing asset bytes and aligns native libraries for 16 KB pages. Keep signing files outside the repository.

See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for design and interaction rules.

Friend requests use their own Android notification channel (independent of opt-in friend-status alerts). The app notifies once per pending requester when the existing mailbox sync finds it, cancels resolved notifications, refreshes requests while foregrounded, and automatically opens pending requests when the app is opened or resumed. Android 0.1.45 wakes mailbox sync through Firebase when a new request arrives, with periodic checks retained as fallback. Inbox counts include pending incoming requests until accepted or declined. Your people expands to an accepted-friends grid or “No friends yet.” The unlocked artwork manager has Simulate friend request and Clear simulation controls: practice state stays on this device, is explicitly labelled, and never creates server friendships or usable parcel recipients. The phone notification test requires Android 0.1.45 and enabled notifications. Its notification now travels through the existing Firebase service to this registered device, rather than being generated directly by the test button. Settings shows the push registration status. A physical-phone test is still required to verify token registration and delivery on the installed device.

Natural beauty in 0.1.45 uses a slightly stronger face-skin blend (30%, previously 22%) and a small increase in teeth whitening. Masks still exclude eyes, brows and lips; original photo dimensions and the saved on/off preference are retained.


Android 0.1.49 gives saved-mail acknowledgements, seen receipts and notification outcomes a separate persistent retry worker. These confirmations do not depend on a full-screen pop-up or DND being turned off. Phone DND is shared with accepted friends as a last-reported setting; Settings → Profile & invitations can disable sharing. The app reads the interruption filter at check-in and never changes DND or requests DND-control access. Changes made while the app is asleep may appear at its next check-in. Physical-device DND and background-delivery testing is still required.
